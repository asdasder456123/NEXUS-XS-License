## 4. Open Source Definition Compliance

NOSL 1.0 is intended to comply with the Open Source Definition.

### OSD 3 — Derived Works

NOSL permits modification and distribution of modified versions.

Its copyleft provisions apply to material derived from or modified from the Covered Work.

Independent works that merely communicate with, use, link to, interoperate with, or are distributed alongside a Covered Work are not automatically treated as Modified Versions.

### OSD 5 — No Discrimination Against Persons or Groups

NOSL does not restrict access to the software based on the identity or status of a person or group.

### OSD 6 — No Discrimination Against Fields of Endeavor

NOSL permits commercial and non-commercial use and does not restrict the field of endeavor in which the software may be used.

The license does not impose ethical-use restrictions or field-specific prohibitions.

### OSD 9 — No Restriction of Other Software

NOSL does not require unrelated software distributed alongside, linked to, communicating with, or interoperating with a Covered Work to be licensed under NOSL solely because of that relationship.

## 7. The Gap NOSL Attempts to Address

NOSL is intended to provide a weak-copyleft model for projects that want modifications to covered software to remain available under the same license while avoiding automatic copyleft obligations for independent software that merely interacts with the covered work.

The license distinguishes between:

1. A Modified Version containing material derived from the Covered Work; and
2. An independent work that merely communicates with, uses, links to, interoperates with, or is distributed alongside the Covered Work.

NOSL also explicitly permits commercial use and does not impose ethical-use restrictions, mandatory fees, telemetry requirements, or approval requirements.

## 8. Comparison With Existing Licenses

### Mozilla Public License 2.0

NOSL shares a weak-copyleft approach with the Mozilla Public License 2.0.

Both licenses allow independent components to remain outside the copyleft scope under specified circumstances.

NOSL uses its own definitions and scope rules for Modified Versions and Covered Works.

MPL-2.0 is an established OSI-approved license and is an important comparison point for NOSL.

### Eclipse Public License 2.0

NOSL also shares characteristics with the Eclipse Public License 2.0, including commercial use and reciprocal obligations for covered modifications.

NOSL differs in its definitions of Modified Version and Covered Work and in its treatment of independent works and interoperability.

### GNU Lesser General Public License

NOSL shares the general goal of allowing covered software to coexist with independent software without automatically applying copyleft to the entire independent work.

NOSL uses its own modification and source-distribution framework rather than adopting the LGPL text.
